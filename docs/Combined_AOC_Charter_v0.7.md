**1. Purpose and Scope**

This charter establishes the Combined AOC: a coordination body that lets independent DCS groups plan, resource, and fly campaigns together without folding into a single organization. It serves as a clearing house — any participating group may post campaign requirements, and any participating group may fill them — and it retains a top-down tasking function (combined targeting, a Combined AOD, and a shared comm plan) for campaigns that need centralized direction.

This charter does not replace a group's own internal structure. A group that already runs its own campaign apparatus, for example its own Campaign Commander and component JFACC, keeps that apparatus exactly as it is. The Combined AOC sits one tier above it, the same way a combined force sits above its national or Service components in real doctrine.

**2. Governing Principles**

These five principles govern every other section of this charter. Where any later section is silent or ambiguous, read it in light of these.

**2.1 Invite-only**

The Combined AOC is not a public, open-enrollment coalition. Groups join by invitation, through the AOC Board, not by open application. This keeps membership deliberate — every group in the coalition was chosen by the groups already in it (Section 4).

**2.2 Minimal interference, with honest concessions**

Every effort will be made not to interfere with a group's normal internal operations, training nights, or schedule. That said, meeting a combined campaign's goals will sometimes require a group to give something up — a scheduling conflict, a requirement claimed that wasn't their first choice, a process step they wouldn't otherwise run. That is an expected cost of participating in something larger, not a failure of this charter, and it should be named as such rather than minimized.

**2.3 Run by the squadrons, for the squadrons**

No single person is in charge of the Combined AOC. The CFC, CFACC, and AOC Board exist because a combined campaign needs someone to hold the pen on a given cycle's products — not because the coalition answers to them. The necessary leadership structure in Section 5 exists to accommodate the goals of the participating groups as fully as possible, not to direct them. This charter is written by the squadrons that make up this coalition, for the squadrons that make up it, and it is amended the same way (Section 14), never by command directive.

**2.4 Built for the lowest common denominator**

Any tool that supports this charter — the Discord registration and claim process in Section 13, DKS integration, or anything on the AOC webpage — is designed first for ease of use. A volunteer with no prior exposure to this charter should be able to post, claim, or check a requirement without having read this document. Complexity is the tool's problem to absorb, not the member's.

**2.5 Communication first**

Communication is a primary objective of this coalition, not a byproduct of it — both in how the AOC runs itself (clear, timely products: the Combined AOD, the comm plan, the ACO) and in what it supports (clear comm plans and briefings for the missions themselves). Where this charter leaves a choice open, default to the option that communicates more, earlier, rather than less.

**3. Doctrinal Basis**

Air Force doctrine already describes this exact situation. When forces are contributed by more than one nation or organization, the functional-component model used for a single force extends directly to combined or coalition employment: the JFACC is redesignated the Combined Force Air Component Commander (CFACC), and the overall commander becomes the Combined Force Commander (CFC).

Doctrine is equally direct about authority in this setting: a commander does not necessarily hold the same degree of control over forces contributed by another organization as over a single force, and that degree of control has to be negotiated. This charter treats that negotiated-control principle as a design requirement, not a limitation — it is what lets a group join a combined campaign without surrendering its own culture, pace, or internal process.

**4. Participation and Component Autonomy**

- Membership is by invitation (Section 2.1). Any DCS group (squadron, wing, or independent unit) may be invited to join the Combined AOC as a participating component; this is not an open-enrollment coalition.

- Each participating group designates one Liaison Officer (LNO) to sit on the AOC Board.

- Participation is voluntary per campaign. A group may sit out a given combined campaign without leaving the coalition.

- A group retains full authority over its own members, schedules, and internal SOP or TTP. The Combined AOC only coordinates what crosses group lines — it does not command within a group.

- No tasking is binding on a group without that group's LNO concurring on its behalf.

**5. Command Structure**

The Combined AOC is organized in four tiers: coalition-wide direction (CFC), combined air direction (CFACC), coordination and consent (the AOC Board), and each group's own retained internal command (component). Holding one of the first two seats is a service rotation, not a position of authority over the coalition (Section 2.3) — each exists to accommodate the participating groups, not to direct them.

**5.1 Combined Force Commander (CFC)**

- Sets the combined campaign's End State, Key Tasks, and priorities — the coalition's own Campaign Intent, held at the combined level alongside (not in place of) each group's own Intent.

- Approves the overall apportionment of combined effort across participating groups, recommended by the CFACC.

- Approves each air-phase transition the CFACC recommends, and publishes the exit-criteria option the CFACC recommends for the campaign (Section 8).

- Final arbiter of disputes the AOC Board cannot resolve by consensus.

- Not involved in daily planning or execution — a low-tempo role that stands above the cycle.

**5.2 Combined Force Air Component Commander (CFACC)**

- Owns the day-to-day running of the Combined AOC: translates the CFC's guidance into combined priorities and the Combined AOD.

- Chairs the AOC Board's planning cycle and approves the Combined AOD and Combined JIPTL before release.

- Recommends the air-phase exit-criteria option before the first cycle, and recommends each air-phase transition when the criteria are met (Section 8).

- Arbitrates planning-level conflicts between groups (e.g., contested resource requests) that do not require CFC involvement.

- Supervises the six functional divisions (Section 5.5).

**5.3 AOC Board**

The Board consists of one LNO from each participating group, with the CFACC as chair. This is the negotiated-control mechanism described in Section 3.

- Reviews and confirms taskings against their group's actual capacity before the Combined AOD is finalized.

- Serves as the conduit for their group's own campaign requirements into the clearing house.

- No tasking is binding on a group without that group's LNO concurrence.

**5.4 Component Commands (each participating group)**

Each group keeps its own internal command chain unchanged. For a group that already runs a full structure, that means its own Campaign Commander, component JFACC, Intel/Assessment lead, Targeting lead, mission designers, Mission Commanders, and AWACS/C2, operating exactly as that group's own procedures describe. A group with a simpler or different internal process is not required to adopt any particular TTP; it only needs to speak the shared interface described in Section 9 when claiming a combined requirement.

> **DESIGN INTENT:** This is what keeps the project from becoming one organization wearing several patches. The Combined AOC coordinates between components; it is not a fifth squadron with authority over the other four.

**5.5 Functional Divisions (under the CFACC)**

Each division is staffed by AOC Board members or their delegates and reports to the CFACC.

| **Division** | **Reports to** | **Function** |
|---|---|---|
| Strategy & Guidance | CFACC | Converts the CFC's combined End State and Key Tasks into cross-group priorities; recommends combined apportionment by group |
| Combat Plans | CFACC | Builds the Combined AOD; nominates and maintains the Combined JIPTL — the clearing-house requirements list each group's LNO can claim against |
| Combat Operations | CFACC | Issues the combined comm plan; tracks claimed requirements through to each group's own execution; handles cross-group retasking |
| ISR | CFACC | Maintains the cross-group threat picture and combined BDA; feeds the next combined cycle |
| Logistics & Resources | CFACC | Tracks what each group can offer (tankers, AWACS, specific airframes, servers, mods) and matches offers to open requirements |
| C4 / Information Management | CFACC | Owns the clearing-house tooling — Discord, DKS, the AOC webpage, and the scripting that collates data between group Discords and the AOC |

**6. Role Rotation**

To keep ownership distributed across the coalition rather than concentrated in one group, the CFC and CFACC seats rotate by campaign:

- A different participating group holds the CFACC seat (and, where the Board elects to combine the roles, the CFC seat) for each new combined campaign.

- The outgoing CFACC hands off standing products — the Combined JIPTL status, the resource ledger, and any open requirements — to the incoming CFACC at the start of the next campaign.

- Rotation order and eligibility (for example, minimum group size or prior AOC experience) are set by Board vote and recorded as an addendum to this charter.

**7. Combined Theater Bible**

Before a combined campaign's Combined Campaign Intent can mean anything, every participating group needs to be flying in the same world — the same enemy, the same political situation, the same map of what's contested and why. The Combined Theater Bible is that shared foundation: a one-time worldbuilding reference, built before Cycle 1 of a combined campaign and revisited only for a major narrative pivot (a neutral state entering the war, a change of starting joint phase), not every cycle.

- Owned jointly by the CFC, who approves it, and the CFACC's Strategy & Guidance and ISR divisions, who draft it.

- Built once per combined campaign using the worksheet in Annex C — belligerent profiles plus the eight PMESII-PT variables (Political, Military, Economic, Social, Information, Infrastructure, Physical Environment, Time), a hybrid threat layer (conventional force, irregular/proxy force, criminal/gray-space actors, neutral/third-party friction), and a "dial sheet" of settings the CFACC can adjust cycle to cycle (enemy aggressiveness, IADS density, ROE sensitivity, and so on) without rewriting the Bible itself.

- No direct one-to-one reference to a real current nation, government, or conflict. Every belligerent is a renamed composite. This is a hard rule, not a style preference — it keeps the campaign fictional for everyone flying it and keeps the coalition out of real-world political territory.

- This is distinct from any Theater Bible a group already keeps for its own single-group campaigns. A group's internal worldbuilding stays theirs; the Combined Theater Bible is canonical only for campaigns run under this charter.

- The combined-level dial sheet (Annex C) is what the CFACC actually touches in the Combined AOD's Part 2 apportionment and ALR guidance (Section 10, Part 2) — the Theater Bible itself does not get rewritten cycle to cycle.

**8. Combined Campaign Intent**

Before a combined campaign's first cycle, the CFC publishes a Combined Campaign Intent on the form in Annex E of 639 CAOC TTP 1-1 (a one-page template is provided): an End State, five standing Key Tasks in priority order, the chosen air-phase exit-criteria option, the apportionment swing threshold, and campaign mechanics (loss tripwire, persistence method, ROE/ALR baseline). The CFACC recommends the exit-criteria option and each later air-phase transition; the CFC publishes the option and approves each transition. The difference at the combined level is scope: this Intent governs what crosses group lines; it does not reach into how any one group runs its own internal cycle.

**9. The Combined Requirements Process**

This is the clearing house. Participating groups post campaign requirements — taskings, resource needs, or capability requests — to the Combined JIPTL for any other participating group to fill.

- Each requirement passes the same three-question test every target already uses internally: which Key Task it serves, what effect it produces, and why it has to happen this cycle.

- Requirements are filled first-come, first-served: any participating group's LNO may claim an open requirement on behalf of their group.

- A claimed requirement is removed from the open board and tracked by Combat Plans/Logistics through to completion. The claiming group then runs it through their own internal process (their own AOD, their own designers and Mission Commanders) exactly as they would one of their own targets.

- Requirements unfilled by a set cutoff ahead of the mission night are flagged to the AOC Board and CFACC for direct assignment or descoping.

- This process runs alongside, not instead of, top-down tasking — the CFACC may still directly assign mission elements when campaign objectives require it.

**10. The Combined AOD**

The Combined AOD is not a new document type. It is the standard Air Operations Directive structure (639 CAOC TTP 1-1, Annex A) with one dimension added: apportionment and tasking are now split by participating group as well as by mission type. A group's own Component AOD may use any form, provided it answers the same blocks.

**Part 1: Combined effects (unchanged from the component AOD, now cross-group)**

- What changed since last cycle — combined BDA across all participating groups

- This cycle's objective — one sentence: effect verb, function, purpose, time

- PIRs — what must be confirmed before combined planning proceeds, by whom, and by when

- Approved cross-group requirements (the Combined JIPTL) — adds a "Claimed by" column naming the group that has taken each item

- Explicitly not this cycle — what is deferred, and why

**Part 2: Combined guidance to component JFACCs**

- Apportionment — now a share of combined effort by participating group, not only by mission category (see Annex A)

- ALR and ROE — a coalition floor the CFACC sets; a component may set a tighter (lower-risk) standard internally but not a looser one without CFACC approval

- TST matrix — targets that pre-empt all other combined tasking, and the accepted risk

- Decision points — what triggers a change in weight of combined effort or in which group leads a given requirement

- Forces available — by group: airframe/role, aircrew signed up, tankers/AWACS/notes

**11. Campaign Cycle (Two-Tier Battle Rhythm)**

The combined cycle wraps each participating group's own internal cycle rather than replacing it. It is built to interfere with a group's normal training and mission nights as little as possible, consistent with Section 2.2 — though claiming a requirement will sometimes still cost a group a scheduling trade-off it would not have made running its own campaign alone.

**11.1 Combined cycle (CFACC)**

- Guidance — CFC/CFACC issue combined objectives and priorities

- Requirement intake — groups post what they need filled and what they can offer

- Combined AOD build — Combat Plans matches requirements to capability and drafts the Combined AOD and comm plan

- Board review & publish — LNOs confirm their group's claimed items; CFACC approves; the Combined AOD is pushed to each group's Discord

- Assess — combined ISR/BDA findings feed the next combined cycle

**11.2 Component cycle (each group, unchanged)**

Each group folds its claimed requirements into its own existing cycle. A typical group cycle runs: Campaign Intent reference, Component AOD, ISR/PIRs, JIPTL, mission planning, fly, feedback/BDA (the 639 CAOC Campaign Cycle and Combined AOD document shows it as Stage B, Steps 1 through 7). Results are reported back up to the combined ISR picture at the end of the step.

**13. Requirement Distribution and the Buy/Pass Process**

This section defines how an open requirement actually reaches a group and comes back claimed — the mechanism behind the clearing-house process in Section 9. It applies identically whether the requirement originated on the current cycle's Combined JIPTL or as a standalone single-mission request; the only difference is where the requirement came from, not how it moves from here on.

**13.1 Registration**

Each participating group registers once with the AOC, through its LNO, and updates the registration whenever its roster changes. The registration record is the routing table — a group is never shown a requirement for an airframe it has not registered.

- Group name/designation, matching the group's AOC Board entry

- Discord server (guild) ID

- AOC distribution channel(s) — the group's own choice; a group flying more than one airframe may route each to a different channel

- Airframe(s)/module(s) flown, each mapped to the channel that should receive requirements for it

- LNO Discord handle — the identity authorized to Buy or Pass on the group's behalf, and who Combat Plans contacts with questions

The registration record is kept in Annex B.

**13.2 The distribution and claim flow**

| **#** | **Step** | **Owner** | **What happens** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Submission | Any group, or CFACC directly | A requirement enters the AOC intake — a Combined JIPTL item from the current cycle, or a standalone single-mission request. Same intake either way. |
| 2 | Review | Combat Plans | Three-question test applied (Key Task, effect, why now). Combat Plans builds the comm plan, ACO, and the ATO line itself, leaving the Tasked Unit field blank. |
| 3 | Distribution | C4 / Information Management | The open ATO line posts to every registered group's channel for the required airframe, and only those channels, with a Buy / Pass prompt. |
| 4 | Response | Group (in their own Discord) | The group's LNO (or a delegate) selects Buy or Pass. The response relays back to Combat Plans' tracking board, timestamped on receipt. |
| 5 | Claim resolution | Combat Plans | First Buy received wins (Section 13.3). The line is marked Claimed, withdrawn from other groups' open boards, and the Tasked Unit field is filled in. |
| 6 | Final posting | Combat Plans | Just before the event, Combat Plans publishes the completed comm plan, ACO, and full ATO — every line's Tasked Unit filled — to all participating groups, not only the one that claimed each line. |

**13.3 Decision Rules**

Four points in the flow above are ambiguous without an explicit rule. These are standing rules under this charter, not left to a case-by-case Board vote:

- Simultaneous Buy. The claim goes to whichever Buy the distribution tool logs first by server-received timestamp, not by when either group's member clicked. A genuine dead-even tie (same logged timestamp) is decided by the CFACC on the spot, favoring whichever group has filled fewer requirements so far this cycle.

- No one buys. An unclaimed line is flagged to the AOC Board and CFACC at T-48h (48 hours before the event) for direct assignment or descoping — inside the T-72h ATO release and ahead of T-24h execution planning, so a directly assigned group still has a full day to plan.

- Backing out of a claim. A Buy locks the claim immediately, but the claiming group's LNO has a 15-minute confirmation window to withdraw a mistaken claim at no cost. After that window, the line is Confirmed and can only be released back to the board through the AOC Board, the same as any other change.

- Lines needing more than one group (for example, a strike package needing a separate SEAD-capable group). These post as separate lines under one shared Package ID rather than a single line requiring two Buys. Each line is independently claimed; Combat Plans groups them by Package ID in the final ATO and briefing.

**13.4 Status Model**

Every ATO line carries one of five statuses, so the distribution tool and Combat Plans always agree on where a line stands:

| **Status** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| Draft | Combat Plans is building the comm plan / ACO / ATO line. Not yet visible to any group. |
| Posted | Distributed to every registered group for the required airframe. Open for Buy / Pass. |
| Claimed | One group has bought it. Withdrawn from other groups' open boards; inside the 15-minute confirmation window. |
| Confirmed | Confirmation window has closed. Tasked Unit field is set. Line is locked to the claiming group. |
| Published | Included in the final mission posting (Section 13.2, Step 6), distributed to all participating groups. |

For the CFC and CFACC's own step-by-step duties in running this process — written for someone new to either seat — see Annex D.

**Every cycle: the CFACC's minimum loop**

The CFC does not run this loop. The CFC is reactive only — see D.4 for exactly when the CFACC should pull the CFC in.

☐ Review last cycle's combined BDA and assessment; update the combined status board (feeds the Combined AOD Part 1 "what changed").

☐ Review the tooling's draft Combined AOD Part 1 (objective, PIRs, "what changed," draft JIPTL). Edit the objective sentence and PIR list so they serve this cycle's Combined Campaign Intent — the tool knows what was submitted, not what the campaign needs.

☐ Apply the three-question test (Section 9) to every item on the draft Combined JIPTL; drop or defer anything that fails it, regardless of what the tool carried forward.

☐ Coordinate with each affected group's own mission designer before locking a requirement's details — feasibility, airframe fit, and timing are theirs to confirm, not the CFACC's to assume.

☐ Edit the tooling's draft Combined AOD Part 2 (apportionment, ALR/ROE floor, TST matrix, decision points) to reflect this cycle's actual priorities. An apportionment swing beyond the threshold set in D.1 goes to the CFC first.

☐ Approve the Combined AOD and release the Combined JIPTL/draft ATO to the distribution tool — Section 13.2's Steps 3 and 4 (distribution and Buy/Pass collection) then run automatically.

☐ Monitor Buy/Pass responses; apply the decision rules (Section 13.3) to ties, no-one-buys lines, and backed-out claims.

☐ Approve the final FragOrder, comm plan, and ATO for publishing to all participating groups (Section 13.2, Step 6).

☐ Close the loop: confirm this cycle's BDA is logged so it is ready as next cycle's "what changed."

Target: under about 90 minutes of staff time per cycle (639 CAOC TTP 1-1, Quick-Start Checklist and Section 13), since the tooling has already done the drafting. If a cycle is taking longer than that, the tooling's inputs (Annex B registration, the Theater Bible dial sheet) are probably stale, not the CFACC.

**D.2 Alignment with 639 CAOC TTP 1-1 (Thinking Like a CFACC)**

639 CAOC TTP 1-1 is the CFACC's working guide to this charter, in full detail. This annex is the short operator's version for someone new to either seat. The two documents are written to interlock, not compete:

- Same vocabulary, same products. JIPTL, AOD, ALR, ROE, and the three-question test mean in the TTP what they mean here (Section 12, Terminology Map). The CFACC's versions of these products only ever reach across group lines (Section 10), never into a group's own internal campaign.

- A group's own component JFACC answers to that group's own Campaign Commander, not to the CFACC. The CFACC can ask a group to fill a combined requirement (Section 9) or set a floor on ALR/ROE (Section 10, Part 2); the CFACC cannot direct how a group's own JFACC runs that group's own targets, air phases, or internal AOD (Section 5.4).

- The TTP's best practices are not repeated in full here: watch execution, don't fly it (TTP 1-1, Section 10.1); the ALR is a ceiling, not a target (Section 8.3); keep the designer and the decision-maker separate (Section 3). Read them there.

- Where this charter is silent, the TTP's doctrine and forms are the fallback. Where they differ, this charter governs.

| **Topic** | **Component JFACC (a group's own)** | **Combined CFC / CFACC (this charter)** |
|---|---|---|
| Owns | Component AOD, JIPTL approval, ALR/ROE, pre-briefed execution calls — for that group's own campaign | Combined AOD, Combined JIPTL approval, ALR/ROE floor, cross-group decision points — only for requirements that cross group lines (Section 10) |
| Cannot do | Change the Campaign Commander's Intent; pick tactics in flight | Direct a group's internal targeting or tactics; override a group's tighter internal ALR/ROE (Section 10, Part 2) |
| Reports to | That group's Campaign Commander | CFACC reports to the CFC; the CFC reports to the AOC Board (Section 5) |
| Builds the AOD/JIPTL from | A blank cycle, by hand | A tooling-generated draft (D.3) that the CFACC edits for the cycle's objectives |

**D.3 What the AOC's Tooling Already Does For You**

Before either role touches a cycle's products, the AOC's Combat Plans / C4 Information Management tooling (Section 13.2) has already produced a complete first draft from the standing Combined Campaign Intent, the Combined Theater Bible's dial sheet (Annex C), and every requirement registered groups have posted. Neither the CFC nor the CFACC builds these from a blank page.

- Draft Combined JIPTL — every open requirement, pre-screened against the registration record (Annex B.1), already in priority order using the same A-D grading the group level uses.

- Draft ATO lines — one per requirement, with the Tasked Unit field left blank until a group Buys it (Section 13.2).

- Draft FragOrder — short-notice taskings assembled from anything posted since the last Combined AOD, ready to post to #frag-orders (Annex B, B.3) once approved.

- Automatic distribution — once approved, the tool posts each ATO line to every registered group's channel for the required airframe and collects Buy/Pass responses without anyone running that step by hand (Section 13.2, Steps 3-4).

> **WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOU:** Your job each cycle is not to create the JIPTL, ATO, or FragOrder — the tooling already has. Your job is to shape what it produced so it actually serves this cycle's Combined Campaign Intent, and to do that in close coordination with the group-level mission designers who will build the real mission files from whatever you approve.

**D.4 CFC Duties**

The CFC is a low-tempo role. It exists to set direction once and arbitrate rarely — not to run cycles. Expect to be pulled in only at the points below:

- A cross-group dispute the AOC Board cannot resolve by consensus (Section 5.3).

- An apportionment swing larger than the threshold set in D.1 (default 15 points, the limit 639 CAOC TTP 1-1 uses): the CFACC recommends, the CFC approves.

- An air-phase transition the CFACC recommends once the exit criteria are met: the CFC approves it. The CFC also publishes the exit-criteria option the CFACC recommends before the first cycle (Section 8).

- A new group's invitation to the coalition (Section 4).

- A major narrative pivot requiring the Combined Theater Bible to be revisited (Section 7).

- The end of a combined campaign, to confirm the next CFC/CFACC rotation (Section 6).

> **COMMON ERROR (SEE 639 CAOC TTP 1-1, SECTION 13):** A CFC who reviews every cycle's Combined JIPTL line by line is doing the CFACC's job and will burn out fast. Set the Combined Campaign Intent once, trust the CFACC to work inside it, and only step in at the triggers above.

**D.5 CFACC Duties**

The CFACC runs the combined cycle day to day. The checklist in D.1 is the job; the eight steps there map directly onto this charter's existing process — BDA and status-board review feeds Section 10, Part 1's "what changed"; the three-question test is the same one used in Section 9; Buy/Pass monitoring and ties are Sections 13.2 and 13.3; and the final publish step is Section 13.2, Step 6. Nothing in this annex creates a new process; it is the operator's checklist for the process this charter already defines.

**Common Failure Modes**

| **Failure** | **Symptom** | **Fix** |
|---|---|---|
| Tooling output accepted unedited | The Combined JIPTL/ATO goes out exactly as generated, with no connection to this cycle's actual objective | Every draft is a draft. Apply the three-question test and the objective sentence before release (D.1) |
| Mission designer bypassed | A requirement gets locked in before the claiming group's own mission designer has confirmed it's buildable | Coordinate with the group-level mission designer before finalizing AOD Part 2 (D.1) |
| CFACC micromanages a claimed requirement | CFACC dictates tactics or routing to a group that bought a requirement | The claiming group runs it through their own internal process (Section 9) — the CFACC's job ends at the Combined AOD |
| CFC reviews every cycle | CFC becomes a bottleneck and burns out | CFC is reactive only (D.4). Set the Intent once and trust the CFACC inside it |
| Apportionment swings go unstated | A large swing appears in the Combined AOD with no stated reason | State the reason in AOD Part 2; swings beyond the threshold need CFC sign-off first (D.1) |

**D.6 Role Handoff Checklist**

Mirrors Section 6 (Role Rotation), phrased as a checklist for the outgoing and incoming CFACC — and CFC, where the Board has combined the seats — at a campaign's end.

- Combined JIPTL status (open, claimed, and confirmed lines) handed to the incoming CFACC.

- The resource ledger and any open requirements handed over, not left to be rediscovered.

- Group Registration Record (Annex B.1) confirmed current before the incoming CFACC's first cycle.

- Combined Theater Bible and current dial-sheet settings (Annex C) briefed to the incoming CFACC.

- Rotation order updated per Board vote, and the next-in-line group notified (Section 6).
