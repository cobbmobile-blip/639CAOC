**THE COMBINED CAMPAIGN CYCLE**

*The 639th CAOC process from scenario setup to assessment, who owns each step, and a detailed look at the Combined AOD that drives every cycle. Companion to AOC Charter 1-1 (Sections 10 and 11) and 639 CAOC TTP 1-1.*

**CHARTER: Authority**

Where this document and the Charter differ, **the Charter governs**. This document adds working detail. It never adds authority. Nothing here is binding on a participating group unless that group's LNO has concurred (Charter Sections 4 and 5.3).

**Part I: The Process**

**Stage A: Scenario setup**

Done once when a combined campaign or a new arc kicks off, not every cycle. Revisit it only when the strategic situation genuinely changes: a new joint phase, reinforcements arriving, or a new arc starting. Stage B assumes Stage A has been answered.

**Step 1. Decide the theater, scope, and starting joint phase**

- Full combined campaign, or a single event to fill a gap between arcs?

- Scale or template (for example, Retribution vs. Pretense, whichever matches the scope): __________

- The theater itself is written up in the **Combined Theater Bible** (Charter Section 7, Annex C). Every belligerent is a renamed composite with no direct stand-in for a real current nation, government, or conflict. This is a hard rule.

"Joint phase" below is the real six-phase joint campaign model (JP 3-0 / JP 5-0), translated to a DCS air campaign. It is the shape of the whole conflict. It is different from Air Phase 1 to 4 in the Combined Campaign Intent, which sequence air effort once combat is underway (joint Phases II and III).

| **Joint phase** | **Name** | **What it looks like in a DCS campaign** |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Shape | Peacetime presence and exercises, no shots fired. Rarely simulated; usually backstory. |
| **I** | Deter | Crisis building: shows of force, QRA intercepts. Red has not struck yet; Blue postures to prevent it. |
| **II** | Seize Initiative | Hostilities just opened: first strikes, the opening air-superiority fight. Most DCS campaigns start here. |
| **III** | Dominate | Sustained major combat. Where Air Phases 1 to 4 (Air Superiority, LOCs, C2, Rear Echelon) mostly play out. |
| **IV** | Stabilize | Major combat has ended: security operations, CAS on call, mopping up remaining resistance. |
| **V** | Enable Civil Authority | Long-tail reconstruction and support. Rarely relevant to a DCS air campaign. |

Starting joint phase: ☐ 0 Shape ☐ I Deter ☐ II Seize Initiative ☐ III Dominate ☐ IV Stabilize ☐ V Enable Civil Authority

Force flow: ☐ Only initial/QRF forces in theater ☐ Secondary or main-line forces arrived ☐ Holding, waiting on reinforcement

**BEST PRACTICE: Opening ALR**

The starting joint phase is a prompt for the opening ALR, not a rule. The ALR source (Seymour, citing AFTTP 3-3) ties MODERATE to Phases I and III (sustained operations, protect the force) and SIGNIFICANT or higher to Phase II (seize the initiative, when the objective is of great importance). Set the real ALR in the Combined AOD from the Intent and the confidence in the enemy order of battle.

**Step 2. Draw the battlefield**

With Step 1 answered, place Blue and Red assets in a realistic disposition. This is the campaign's starting order of battle, and it follows from the joint phase and force flow you picked. A Phase II start means a thin, patchy Blue presence and an enemy still holding the initiative. A Phase III start means hardened positions, a real FLOT, and logistics tails on both sides. A Phase IV start means security-focused dispositions with most major combat power already spent. Do not place forces because they would be interesting to fight; place them where they honestly would be. **Save this as the frozen baseline target list, dated before Cycle 1.** Air phase exit criteria are measured against it. The CFACC's Strategy & Guidance and ISR divisions draft it and the CFC approves it (Charter Annex D.1).

**Step 3. Reality-check against what the coalition can actually fly**

Cross-reference the order of battle against the **Group Registration Record** (Charter Annex B.1): each group's airframes, mods, home bases, how many aircrew typically sign up, and who its LNO is. A paper order of battle is worthless if nobody can fly it. Adjust the map to match reality, not the other way around. Logistics & Resources keeps this picture current.

**Step 4. Stand up the staff and the standing products**

Use the Quick-Start Checklist in TTP 1-1 and Charter Annex D.1. Fill the CFC and CFACC seats (Charter Section 6), seat the AOC Board with one LNO per group, and staff the six divisions with whoever you have, since one person may wear several hats. Publish the standing products once: Combined Campaign Intent, basic SPINS, ROE, CDE criteria, ALR matrix, comm matrix, ACO, and the status board. If you use a persistence tool (Retribution or Pretense), note in the Intent which of its own state-saving decides what effects carry over, so the Combined AOD uses only effect verbs the campaign can honor.

**Stage B: The recurring cycle**

The combined cycle wraps each group's own internal cycle. It does not replace it (Charter Section 11). Once Stage A is answered, this repeats every cycle. The Combined AOD gets its own walkthrough in Part II.

**B.1 Combined cycle (CFACC)**

| **#** | **Step** | **Owner** | **What happens** | **Gate / product** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Guidance | CFC sets; CFACC and Strategy & Guidance | The CFC's Combined Campaign Intent stands (End State, Key Tasks, air phase criteria, weight of effort). Each cycle the CFACC issues priorities for this cycle. Set once, referenced every cycle. | Signed Intent; CFACC guidance in #cfacc-guidance |
| 2 | Requirement intake | Each group's LNO; Logistics & Resources | Groups post what they need filled and what they can offer. Every requirement passes the three-question test. Offers (tankers, AWACS, airframes) are logged. | Requirements and offers on the board |
| 3 | Combined AOD build | Combat Plans, ISR; CFACC edits | BDA, this cycle's objective, and PIRs drive the draft. Combat Plans matches requirements to capability, sorts them by Key Task and grade A to D, and drafts the Combined JIPTL and comm plan. ISR answers the PIRs first. | Draft Combined AOD (Parts 1 and 2); draft Combined JIPTL |
| 4 | Board review and publish | LNOs confirm; CFACC approves; C4/IM distributes | LNOs confirm their group's claimed items. The CFACC approves, then the Combined AOD and Combined JIPTL go to each group's Discord and **Buy/Pass** runs (Charter Section 13). Swings over the threshold go to the CFC first. | Approved Combined AOD; Combined JIPTL (freeze line); ATO lines Claimed, Confirmed, Published |
| 5 | Assess | ISR, Combat Operations; CFACC | Combined BDA and AARs update the status board. The CFACC tests the air phase exit criteria and, if met, recommends a transition. The CFC approves it. Loops to Step 1. | Combined BDA log; air phase recommendation |

**DECISION POINT: Air phase check**

At Step 5 the CFACC compares the status board to the exit-criteria option chosen in the Combined Campaign Intent. If met, the CFACC recommends an air phase transition in the next Combined AOD. **The CFC approves it.**

**Timeline against the mission night**

The Charter fixes three marks (Section 13.3 and Annex D). **T-72h:** the ATO is released and requirements are open for claim. **T-48h:** any line nobody has Bought is flagged to the AOC Board and CFACC for direct assignment or descoping. **T-24h:** a group is in execution planning, so a directly assigned group still has a full day to plan. A group that cannot meet these marks tells its LNO early. The cycle is built to cost a group as little of its own training night as possible (Charter Section 2.2).

**B.2 Component cycle (each group, unchanged)**

Each group folds the requirements it Bought into its **own** cycle, run by its own component JFACC and Campaign Commander. For a group using TTP 1-1 that is the seven steps below. The combined level never reaches into it, and results are reported back up to the combined ISR picture at the end of the last step.

| **#** | **Component step** | **Owner (inside the group)** |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Campaign Intent (standing) | Group's Campaign Commander |
| 2 | Component AOD (BDA, objective, PIRs; Part 2 as needed) | Group's component JFACC |
| 3 | ISR / PIRs | Group's Intel |
| 4 | JIPTL for the group's own targets, plus Bought combined requirements | Intel drafts; component JFACC approves |
| 5 | Mission planning (SPINS, Mission Brief Checklist, products guide) | Designers and Mission Planners |
| 6 | Fly the missions (a retask of a Bought requirement needs the group's LNO concurrence) | Mission Planners, AWACS |
| 7 | Feedback / BDA, reported up to the combined ISR picture | Intel, component JFACC |

*Stage A is revisited only when something in it would change: a new arc, a shift in the joint phase, or reinforcements arriving. Otherwise every cycle just loops through Stage B.*

**Part II: The Combined AOD in Detail**

**Definition**

The Combined Air Operations Directive is how the CFACC translates the CFC's standing Combined Campaign Intent into current guidance for the whole coalition. It is not a new document type. It is each group's own AOD with one dimension added: **apportionment and tasking are split by participating group** as well as by mission type (Charter Section 10). It does not rewrite the Intent. It answers a narrower question: given what has changed and where we are, what should combined air power focus on this cycle, and why?

One "cycle" is roughly one campaign session or week. A blank form alone does not teach anyone how to think about it, so this part shows what a properly filled-out Combined AOD looks like. The blank form is **Charter Annex A**, which is the same form as TTP 1-1 Annex A.

**What it contains**

| **Part** | **Block** | **Question it answers** |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Air phase and cycle | Where are we, and which exit-criteria option are we measuring against? |
| **1** | What changed (combined BDA) | What was confirmed, probable, or unconfirmed across all groups? What is new? |
| **1** | This cycle's combined objective | What specific effect, and why now? [effect verb + function + purpose + time] |
| **1** | PIRs | What must we confirm first, who collects, and by when? |
| **1** | Approved cross-group requirements (Combined JIPTL) | What passed the three-question test, with effect, Task, grade A to D, MOE, and **which group claimed it**? |
| **1** | Explicitly not this cycle | What is deferred, and why? |
| **2** | Apportionment by group | Each group's share of this cycle's combined effort, and a one-line reason for any change (up to the swing threshold, default 15 points). |
| **2** | ALR and ROE (coalition floor), TST matrix | How much risk per function? Which targets pre-empt everything? Groups may tighten the floor, never loosen it without CFACC approval. |
| **2** | Decision points, forces available by group | What triggers a change or a change of lead group? Who is actually flying? |

Each block only makes sense in terms of the one before it: BDA drives the objective, the objective drives the PIRs, the PIRs drive ISR, and only then do requirements get approved.

**What it is not**

- **Not** a rewrite of the Combined Campaign Intent. That is standing.

- **Not** a way to direct how a group runs its own targets, air phases, or internal AOD. The Combined AOD reaches only what crosses group lines (Charter Section 5.4).

- **Not** a list of whatever requirements look interesting to fly this week.

- **Not** optional paperwork. It is the mechanism that makes the three-question test happen instead of staying theoretical.

- It **is** a short, honest answer to "what does this campaign need next, and why."

- It **is** the only place a cross-group requirement gets permission to exist on this cycle's Combined JIPTL.

**Quick reference: the Key Tasks**

Every Combined AOD cites "Task 1," "Task 2," and so on. These are the five standing Key Tasks from the Combined Campaign Intent, in priority order, set once and never redefined week to week.

| **Task** | **Name** | **In one line** |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Air superiority (main effort in Air Phase 1; floor after) | Win control of the air first. Everything else waits on this. |
| **2** | Interdict LOCs | Cut roads, rail, and bridges to choke Red's resupply and reinforcement. |
| **3** | Destroy C2 nodes | Kill Red's headquarters and comms so the response falls apart. |
| **4** | Attrit rear echelon | Grind down depots, staging areas, and reserves once the above are underway. |
| **5** | Assist front-line units | On-call CAS for troops in contact. Continuous, with no target tier of its own. |

**Conditional approval: when a target has no grid yet**

A requirement can be approved before ISR has located it, but only with all three of these:

- A PIR assigned to find it, with an asset and a due date

- A fallback decision point that says what happens if the PIR is not answered in time

- Nothing in flight that depends on it before the PIR is answered

**Worked example: Cycle 3**

A filled-out Combined AOD using a fictional campaign. Compare it with Charter Annex A. All grids, place names, results, and numbers are notional.

| **Cycle #** | **Date** | **Current air phase and exit option** |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | [session date] | 1: Air Superiority (contested). Option 2, Named effects. Not yet cleared to shift weight toward LOCs. |

**What changed since last cycle**

*Cycle 2 confirmed destruction of the EWR site at XX1234. The SA-11 battery at XX5566 survived and relocated about 3 km south; the new position is unconfirmed. Red flew a noticeably higher CAP tempo out of Northvale Airbase, suggesting reinforcement rather than attrition of its fighters. Two of three groups reported SAM launches; Group C reported none.*

**This cycle's combined objective**

*Destroy the relocated SA-11 battery so it cannot engage packages in the Task 1 strike corridor, and hold the corridor once open. Task 2 stays on hold until the SAM is confirmed dead; hitting the rail yard at Eastmark now would put strike aircraft under a SAM we cannot currently see.*

**PIRs for this cycle**

| **PIR** | **Asset / method** | **Due** |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Exact position of the relocated SA-11 battery | Recon flight with TGP (Group B), ahead of the planning session | T-4d |
| 2. Has a second SAM system moved into the sector? | Same recon flight; Tacview review of Cycle 2 | T-4d |
| 3. Current fighter strength at Northvale Airbase | AWACS orbit (Group A) extended 1 hour to track Red CAP | Before planning |

*Results are due before combined planning proceeds, not during it.*

**Approved cross-group requirements (the Combined JIPTL)**

| **Requirement** | **Task / Grade** | **Effect** | **Claimed by** | **MOE** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SA-11 battery (relocated; grid pending ISR) **CONDITIONAL** | Task 1 / A | Destroy; opens the corridor Task 2 needs next cycle | Group B (Buy confirmed) | Battery gone, or no launches on the package (Tacview) |
| Northvale Airbase CAP (standing OCA/DCA task, not a point target) | Task 1 / B | Prevent Red re-contesting cleared airspace | Group A | No Red fighter reaches the corridor |
| Corridor SEAD escort for the SA-11 strike (Package ID 3-07) | Task 1 / A | Suppress; protect the strike package | Group C | No SAM-caused loss |

*Conditional on PIR 1 by T-4d. If the SA-11 is not located, see the decision point in Part 2. Lines 1 and 3 share a Package ID, so they are claimed separately but briefed together.*

**Explicitly not this cycle**

*Rail yard at Eastmark (Task 2 / Grade B). It is a legitimate requirement and stays on the standing list, but striking it now risks strike aircraft under a SAM that has not been relocated. Revisit once the Task 1 objective above is confirmed complete.*

**Part 2: Apportionment by group**

| **Group** | **Air Phase 1 default %** | **This cycle %** | **Reason if changed (max 15 pts)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Group A (fighters) | 40 | 40 | — |
| Group B (SEAD and strike) | 35 | 40 | +5: SEAD needed to hunt the relocated SA-11 |
| Group C (strike and CAS) | 25 | 20 | −5: no strike goes in until the SA-11 is found |

*Applies to sorties actually signed up. The change is 5 points, under the swing threshold, so no CFC sign-off is needed. A 15-point-plus change would go to the CFC first.*

**Part 2: ALR, ROE, TST, decision point, forces**

| **Item** | **Direction** |
|---|---|
| **ALR (coalition floor)** | SEAD: HIGH. Strikers: MEDIUM. Tankers and AWACS: LOW. Troops-in-contact CAS: HIGH. Groups may go lower, not higher, without CFACC approval. |
| **ROE** | Default WEAPONS FREE in the AO; TIGHT in international waters and friendly territory. No override this cycle. Dial sheet unchanged. |
| **TST** | Troops-in-contact CAS (Task 5): standing, ALR HIGH. Mobile SA-11 launcher if located: Neutralize, ALR HIGH. |
| **Decision point** | If PIR 1 is not answered by T-4d, Group B's planners switch the package to a SEAD-led sweep with no strike into the corridor, and the SA-11 requirement stays open next cycle. LTIOV: T-4d. |
| **Forces available** | [from sign-ups, by group: aircrew by airframe, tanker track, AWACS crewed or not] |

Notice every line traces back to something: the objective explains the requirements, the requirements explain the PIRs, the excluded item has a stated reason, every apportionment change has a one-line reason, and every line names who took it. That traceability is the entire point of the Combined AOD.

**BDA wording**

| **Status** | **Meaning** | **Use** |
|---|---|---|
| **Confirmed** | Sensor, video, or Tacview evidence | Update the board; counts toward air phase exit |
| **Probable** | Strong indicators, not verified | Assign a PIR to confirm; does not count |
| **Unconfirmed** | Damage suspected, no evidence | Treat as active until collected |

Crews report through the in-flight report and AAR formats in the SPINS (callsign, mission number, target location, time on target, results, remarks) and post BDA to **#combined-aar-bda**. Positions use the SPINS format so ISR can paste them straight into planning tools.
