**C. Every cycle: the minimum loop**

- **ISR: update the combined status board from last event's AARs and Tacview; list what changed**

- **CFACC: edit the tooling's draft Combined AOD Part 1 (objective, PIRs, approved requirements, not-this-cycle). Edit Part 2 (apportionment by group, ALR/ROE floor, TST, decision points) only for items that changed. A swing past the threshold goes to the CFC first**

- **ISR: answer the PIRs before group designers lock routes and timing**

- **Combat Plans: compile the draft Combined JIPTL from every source: requirement posts, re-attack requests, and ISR recommendations from BDA**

- **CFACC: run the three-question test on every requirement, confirm feasibility with each affected group's designer, then approve the Combined JIPTL and release it to distribution**

- **Combat Operations / C4: monitor Buy/Pass; apply the decision rules to ties, backed-out claims, and lines nobody bought (T-48h); reissue the ACO or comm matrix only if something changed**

- **CFACC: approve the final FragOrder, comm plan, and ATO lines; before the event, answer group feasibility calls: scrub, add assets, or raise ALR**

- **Execution: CFACC watches through AWACS and acts only at pre-briefed decision points; retasking a claimed requirement needs the claiming group's LNO concurrence**

- **After: collect AARs and BDA (#combined-aar-bda); update the board; start the next Combined AOD**

*Target: under 90 minutes of staff time per cycle once the Section B products exist and the tooling is drafting. If a cycle runs longer, the registration data or the dial sheet is probably stale (Charter Annex D.1). The CFC does not run this loop (Section 3.3).*

**D. Keep the campaign small**

- **Air Phases:** Annex E Option 3 is the shortest (about 8 events). For a shorter campaign, merge air phases (for example, air superiority, then LOCs plus C2, then deep targets) and cap each air phase at 2 cycles.

- **Combined JIPTL: approve 3 to 5 requirements per cycle. If everything is priority A, nothing is.**

- **ALR:** one campaign ALR per air phase plus the function table. No per-mission overrides unless a TST or a decision point triggers one.

**1. Purpose and Scope**

This TTP tells the designated CFACC, and everyone who feeds or follows the CFACC, how to turn the CFC's standing Intent into a weekly combined air plan where every target has a reason. It keeps the core of the original primer: **one extra question, asked once per cycle, before targets are handed to designers.** It adds the doctrine behind that question, the workflow to run it across a coalition of volunteer DCS groups, and the forms to make it stick.

**Applies to:** the CFACC, the CFC, the AOC Board and each group's LNO, the six functional divisions (Strategy & Guidance, Combat Plans, Combat Operations, ISR, Logistics & Resources, C4/IM), each group's own designers and Mission Commanders (MCs), and AWACS/C2. It works alongside each group's own tactics publications, which this TTP neither replaces nor reaches into.

**Relationship to AOC Charter 1-1.** The Charter governs organization, authority, and process. This TTP is the CFACC's working guide to it, and the fallback only where the Charter is silent. Where they differ, the Charter wins and this TTP should be corrected. Terminology follows Charter Section 12.

**Terms used here.** "AOD" means the Combined AOD (Part 1 combined effects, Part 2 guidance to component JFACCs) and "JIPTL" means the Combined JIPTL, unless a group's own Component AOD or JIPTL is named. "Component JFACC" is a group's own internal role. It answers to that group's own Campaign Commander, never to the CFACC. "The Intent" is the Combined Campaign Intent.

**BEST PRACTICE: Steal the logic, not the staff**

A real Air Operations Center employs on the order of two thousand people (Kometer, *Command in Air War*). We are not building that. Keep the logic of the tasking cycle (guidance, effects, targets, allocation, tasking, assessment) and shrink the staff to one form, one list, and one board.

**2. The Idea: Two Mindsets**

An aircrew mindset asks "what can I hit this mission?" A CFACC mindset asks "what does the campaign need to happen next, and does this target produce it?" Both are necessary. If only the first runs the show, targets get picked because they are available or fun to fly, and three cycles later nobody can explain why the comm tower or bunker complex was destroyed.

**2.1 The chain of logic**

Every strike traces up this chain. A target that cannot be traced up it does not go on this cycle's list, however good it looks on the map.

| **End State** | **Key Tasks** | **This Cycle's Effect** | **Targets** | **Missions** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Set once, in the Combined Campaign Intent | Standing 1 to 5 priority order | What has to happen now, given where the campaign is | Chosen to produce that effect | How aircrew execute it |
| CFC | CFC | **CFACC** | Combat Plans, with each claiming group's designers; CFACC approves | Each group's designers, MCs, flights |

The first two links are already written in the Intent and do not change week to week (starter Key Tasks and air phase criteria are in Annex E). The last two are what designers naturally think about. The **missing middle link**, "given where the campaign is right now, what specific effect do we need this cycle?", is what the CFACC owns.

**2.2 How this maps to doctrine**

| **Our term** | **Doctrinal term** | **Source** |
|---|---|---|
| **End State (Intent)** | JFC objectives and end state; JFACC operational objectives (OOs) with measures of effectiveness (MOE) | JOPPA; JP 3-56.1 |
| **Key Tasks 1 to 5** | Essential tasks / OOs, in priority order; the basis for apportionment | JOPPA Step 2; JP 3-56.1 |
| **This cycle's effect** | Tactical objectives (TOs) and tactical tasks (TTs), published in the Air Operations Directive (AOD) | JOPPA Step 3; AFDP tasking cycle, Stage 1 |
| **Targets** | Target nomination list (TNL) leading to the JIPTL | AFDP tasking cycle, Stage 2 |
| **Missions** | MAAP, then ATO and SPINS | AFDP tasking cycle, Stages 3 and 4 |

JOPPA calls this the strategy-to-task hierarchy: OOs, then TOs, then TTs, "logically linking all air activity (strategy to tasks to targeting)." Our five links are the same idea with the staff work compressed.

**2.3 Who decides what: centralized control, decentralized execution**

Current Air Force doctrine frames this as Centralized Command, Distributed Control, Decentralized Execution under mission command (AFDP 3-0), and treats control over multinational forces as negotiated, something less than full OPCON. The CAOC uses the negotiated version. The CFACC directs what crosses group lines. A group keeps full authority over its own members, schedules, and internal procedures, and no tasking binds a group unless its LNO concurs (Charter Sections 4 and 5.3).

| **The CFACC decides** | **The group, its MCs, and flight leads decide** |
|---|---|
| **What effect, why now, what share of combined effort each group carries, how much risk (the ALR/ROE floor) for the cycle, and which requirements are posted for claim. Only what crosses group lines.** | **Whether and how a group fills a requirement: its own air phases and Component AOD, routing, timing within the window, tactics, formation, attack and re-attack within ALR. A tighter internal ALR always stands.** |

**COMMON ERROR: Both extremes lose**

Kometer's history of air control shows both failure modes: airpower broken into "penny packets" (Vietnam route packages) and central staffs micromanaging details. A CFACC who writes ingress altitudes into the AOD is doing the MC's job. A CFACC who publishes no priorities is doing nobody's.

**BEST PRACTICE: Ask first, then task**

The CFACC and CFC seats are a service rotation, not a position of authority over the coalition (Charter Section 2.3). If a group's LNO declines a requirement, that is a planning input, not insubordination: re-scope it, descope it, or offer it to another group.

**3.1 Scaling by team size**

| **Active aircrew per event (all groups)** | **How to staff it** |
|---|---|
| **Up to about 6** | CFACC also runs ISR and plans. One designer per group. Use Combined AOD Part 1 only (Annex A). One package per event. |
| **About 7 to 15** | Separate ISR lead. Combat Plans can be the CFACC's deputy. Designers split by mission type. Use the full Combined AOD (Parts 1 and 2). |
| **16 or more** | Add a deputy CFACC for execution week and a weekly 15-minute Board sync of all LNOs (doctrine's JTCB idea: one forum where every mission owner states priorities before requirements lock). |

**COMMON ERROR: Designer knowledge leak**

A designer who places the SAM sites knows where they are. If that designer then flies or briefs the mission, your threat picture is fiction and PIRs are theater.

**Fix:** designers do not fly the missions they built. If unavoidable, the intel lead briefs the threat at the confidence level intel would honestly have, and the designer stays quiet about the EOB.

**BEST PRACTICE: Keep the CFACC out of the cockpit**

The CFACC job is decision-heavy and flight-light. Do not schedule the CFACC as strike lead in an event they are running. In execution, the CFACC watches the picture from the C2 channel and acts only through AWACS, and only at pre-briefed decision points (Section 10).

**3.2 Rotation and handoff**

The CFACC seat rotates by campaign: a different participating group holds it for each new combined campaign, and the Board may combine it with the CFC seat. Rotation order and eligibility are set by Board vote (Charter Section 6). The outgoing CFACC hands over standing products, not just the title. Use the role handoff checklist in Section 14.

**3.3 When the CFACC pulls in the CFC**

The CFC is reactive only. Pull the CFC in for: a cross-group dispute the Board cannot settle by consensus; an apportionment swing larger than the threshold; a new group's invitation to the coalition; a major narrative pivot that reopens the Combined Theater Bible; the end of a campaign, to confirm the next rotation (Charter D.4). Each air phase transition the CFACC recommends is also approved by the CFC, and the CFC publishes the exit-criteria option (Charter Sections 5.1, 5.2, 8 and D.4).

**4. The Combined Cycle**

Doctrine describes a six-stage tasking cycle (JP 3-56.1 and the AFDP tasking cycle): objectives, effects and guidance; target development; weaponeering and allocation; ATO production; execution planning and force execution; assessment. Real AOCs run it on a 24-hour ATO day. We run it once per event, in two tiers. The combined cycle the CFACC runs (Charter Section 11.1) wraps each group's own component cycle (Section 11.2) and does not replace it. The stages are the same; the clock is stretched.

Times below are counted back from the event start (T0). T-72h, T-48h, and T-24h are the Charter's marks: ATO release, unfilled-line flag, and execution planning (Section 13.3). The T-4d and earlier marks are this TTP's own reading of when the Combined AOD is approved. Stretch or squeeze to your cadence.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T+0 to T+2d** | Assessment | AARs, in-flight reports, and Tacview from every participating group are collected (#combined-aar-bda). Combined status board updated. Re-attack needs are identified. | ISR | Combined BDA log, status board |
| **T-6d** | Guidance and requirement intake | CFACC issues guidance and writes Combined AOD Part 1: what changed, this cycle's objective, PIRs. Group LNOs post requirements and offers. | CFACC; LNOs | AOD Part 1; requirements on the board |
| **T-6d to T-4d** | ISR against PIRs | Collection tasked and flown or reviewed. Threat overlay and target folders updated. Nothing is planned against a picture that has not been refreshed. | ISR | PIR answers, updated EOB |

| **T-4d** | Board review and approval | LNOs confirm their group's items against capacity. CFACC approves requirements and the "explicitly not this cycle" list. **BEST PRACTICE: The freeze line**

Doctrine lets components submit critical changes to target requests and asset availability during ATO production, but the cycle only works if most changes wait. After the freeze line, accept only changes that meet this test: without it, an A-priority effect fails or a safety issue appears. A Confirmed line can be released back to the board only through the AOC Board (Charter Section 13.3). Everything else goes to the next cycle. This protects every group's designers' evenings.

**DECISION POINT: How often to issue the AOD**

Doctrine expects an AOD daily. A coalition flying weekly does one Combined AOD per event. If you fly twice a week, run one Combined AOD for the pair and note which requirement effects belong to which event.

**4.1 The component tier**

Inside the combined cycle, each group runs its own cycle unchanged: its Campaign Commander's Intent, its Component AOD, its own ISR, its own JIPTL for its own targets, mission planning, flight, and BDA. The group folds Bought requirements into that cycle exactly as it would one of its own targets (Charter Section 9) and reports results back up to the combined ISR picture. A group using this TTP at its own scale reads "CFACC" as its component JFACC. A group with a simpler process need only speak the shared interface: the Buy/Pass response, the Package ID, and BDA to #combined-aar-bda. The full two-tier walkthrough is in the Combined Campaign Cycle document.

**5. The Combined Air Operations Directive (Combined AOD)**

The AOD is "the primary vehicle for communicating desired effects to target developers and others involved in the tasking process" (AFDP tasking cycle). The strategy staff drafts it for CFACC approval. It also carries the apportionment decision into the planning cell. In the OPAR SPINS, the CFACC also uses the AOD to override default weapons status for an ATO day.

The Combined AOD is not a new document type. It is each group's own AOD with one dimension added: apportionment and tasking are split by participating group as well as by mission type (Charter Section 10). It has two parts. Part 1 is the combined effects form. Part 2 is guidance to component JFACCs. Both are in Annex A.

| **Block** | **Question it answers** | **Part** |
|---|---|---|
| **Cycle, date, air phase** | Where are we in the campaign? | 1 |
| **What changed** | What did combined BDA confirm across all groups, what is still active, what is new? (Feeds directly from last cycle's assessment.) | 1 |
| **This cycle's objective** | What specific effect, and why now? Every requirement traces to this sentence. | 1 |
| **PIRs** | What must we confirm before planning? Who collects, by when? | 1 |
| **Approved requirements (the Combined JIPTL)** | What passed the three-question test, with effect, task, grade, MOE, and which group claimed it? The CFACC approves this list. | 1 |
| **Explicitly not this cycle** | What is tempting but deferred, and why? | 1 |
| **Apportionment by group** | What share of this cycle's combined effort does each group carry, and how is it split by mission category? | 2 |
| **ALR and ROE (coalition floor)** | How much risk per mission type? What weapons status and restrictions? A group may tighten but not loosen without CFACC approval. | 2 |
| **TST matrix** | What targets pre-empt all other tasking if found, and at what accepted risk? | 2 |
| **Restrictions** | Restricted Target List and No-Strike List changes. | 2 |
| **Dial sheet line** | Does this cycle's setting differ from the Theater Bible baseline (for example ROE / collateral sensitivity)? | 2 |
| **Decision points** | What triggers a change in weight of effort, a branch, or which group leads? | 2 |
| **Forces available by group** | Who and what is actually flying (sign-ups by group and airframe, tankers, AWACS)? | 2 |

**5.1 Writing the objective sentence**

Use one sentence: **[effect verb] + [enemy function or system] + [purpose] + [time].** Take the verb from the effects list the OPAR SPINS defines for the ATO, and be honest about what it means in DCS.

| **Verb** | **Meaning (OPAR SPINS 5.4)** | **What it means in a DCS campaign** |
|---|---|---|
| **Destroy** | Cannot function or be restored; permanent, all aspects | Units removed and **stay removed** in later mission files |
| **Degrade** | Permanent but partial; still operating, not fully | Designer reduces the site (fewer launchers, one radar less) in later missions |
| **Neutralize** | Ineffective for a specific period | Site cannot engage during the package window (SEAD effect) |
| **Disrupt** | Temporary; only portions affected | Delay or distraction; effect gone by next event |
| **Deny** | Hinder enemy use of space or facilities; temporary but total | Runway, bridge, or road closed for this mission or a defined number of cycles |
| **Attrit** | Destroy by firepower (troops) | Percent of a named force killed; state the percent |
| **Harass** | Curtail movement, lower morale by threat of loss | Reduce enemy movement in an area; hard to measure, use sparingly |
| **Prevent** | Keep from happening | Stop a specific enemy action (counterattack, resupply); state the trigger |

**Weak:** "Strike the comm tower at XX4455." **Better:** "**Destroy** the comm tower at XX4455, which relays targeting data to the SA-11 battery covering Corridor BLUE, so that the battery cannot cue on Saturday's strike package (Task 1)."

**DCS NOTE: Effects need persistence**

In DCS, an effect is only real if the **next mission file reflects it**. "Destroy" with no carry-over is just "disrupt for one evening." Decide at AOD time how each effect will persist (Section 12).

**5.2 Apportionment vs allocation**

**Apportionment is the CFACC's recommendation of how much air effort goes to each objective or mission type over the period, usually as a percentage or a priority order (JFC approval in doctrine; here the CFC approves only a swing beyond the threshold). At combined scope it is also split by group. Allocation is the distribution of actual forces against that plan: who flies which package. The Combined AOD sets apportionment. Buy/Pass, each group's designers, and the MAAP do allocation.**

In a coalition the effort you apportion is sorties from aircrew who signed up in each group, not airframes. Count sign-ups by group first (the Registration Record and the tooling have them), apportion second. A plan that assumes twelve flights when six will show is a plan with no priorities.

Start from the standing weight-of-effort table for the current air phase (Annex E.5). The CFACC may move any category up to the swing threshold (default 15 points) with a one-line reason in Part 2. A larger swing goes to the CFC first. Then split the result by group.

**Target priority grade (OPAR SPINS 5.3)**

| **Grade** | **Meaning** | **Three-question test link** |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Essential to current objectives, or a designated high-value / high-payoff / time-sensitive target. Timeliness may not exist later. | Passes Q1 and Q2 and **must happen this cycle** |
| **B** | Substantial but not immediate. Costs more effort if left for later. | Passes Q1 and Q2; better now, survivable later |
| **C** | Contributes but not critical. Will eventually need targeting. | Passes Q1 and Q2; **nice to have** |
| **D** | Target of opportunity or backup if others are unsuitable. | Backup only; keep off the primary list |

**6. Target-to-Effect: The Three-Question Test**

Before a target goes on the list, the nominator answers all three:

1. **Which Key Task does this serve?** Cite Task 1 to 5 from the Intent. (Strategy-to-task trace.)

2. **What effect happens if we succeed?** Use an effect verb and a measure. "We blew something up" is not an effect. (Effect and MOE.)

3. **Does it have to happen this cycle, or could it wait?** If nothing depends on it now, it is grade C or D, not this cycle's target. (Priority grade.)

Doctrine adds a fourth thing the tasking cycle requires at nomination time: **"How will we know we have achieved the desired effect?"** Collection and exploitation requirements are set with the target, not afterward. The Requirement Nomination Card (Annex B) has a post-strike collection line for exactly this reason.

**6.1 Choosing the right node: draw the system**

JOPPA's operational design stresses modelling the enemy as **nodes and links** rather than as a list of things to shoot. Nodes are tangible (radars, SAM batteries, command posts, depots). Links are what connects them (data relay, roads, power, supply). Often the best target is the link.

A ten-minute sketch of the enemy air defense network answers most nomination arguments: early warning radar feeds the sector operations node, which cues the SAM battery through a comm relay. Killing the relay (Destroy) or the battery (Neutralize during the window) both serve Task 1. Killing an unrelated bunker does not.

**BEST PRACTICE: One diagram per air phase**

Keep one whiteboard or slide with the enemy air defense and C2 network for the current air phase. Update it after each assessment. Ask of every nomination: "Where does this sit on the diagram?"

**6.2 Ends, ways, means, risk**

When more than one path reaches the effect, JOPPA suggests comparing options on four lines: **ends** (which objectives, how completely), **ways** (which air phase and tasks), **means** (how much effort), **risk**. Two options on one small table takes two minutes and prevents the default "hit whatever is closest." Example: option one suppresses the SAM directly with SEAD; option two blinds it by killing the comm link. The second may cost fewer sorties but depends on a PIR you have not answered.

**6.3 Restrictions and legal discipline**

Doctrine keeps two lists next to the target list. The **No-Strike List** protects objects under the law of armed conflict or ROE. The **Restricted Target List** holds targets that need higher approval before action (political, cultural, or friendly-operations reasons). The tasking-cycle guidance also notes that dual-use objects such as power or telecommunications must be weighed against military benefit. That is worth playing straight even in a sim: a comm tower serving both the SA-11 network and a town is a decision, not a freebie. Strictness follows the dial sheet in the Combined Theater Bible (ROE / collateral sensitivity); note a change in Part 2 rather than editing the Bible mid-campaign.

In the CAOC: Combat Plans maintains the NSL; the CFC (or the approver the Combined Campaign Intent names) approves RTL entries; changes go in Combined AOD Part 2.

**7. PIRs and Collection in DCS**

**BDA and this cycle's objective generate this cycle's PIRs.** PIRs are the specific things you must confirm before planning intelligently: has that SAM moved, is the bridge still down, what is defending the C2 node. Task collection against them first. Mission planning starts after the answers, so routes, timing, and threat calls rest on a real picture.

**7.1 CCIRs: PIR and FFIR**

Doctrine groups the commander's decision-driving questions as **CCIRs**: priority intelligence requirements (enemy and environment) and friendly force information requirements (our own forces). JOPPA stresses that CCIRs "belong exclusively to the commander" and exist to support specific decisions, often changes in weight of effort, branches, or phase shifts. Each should carry a latest-time-information-of-value (LTIOV).

| **Type** | **Example** | **Decision it supports** | **LTIOV** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PIR** | Has the SA-11 battery moved from its last confirmed location? | Go/no-go on the comm-tower strike; retarget to new site | T-4d |
| **PIR** | Is the bridge on MSR North still passable? | Whether Interdiction stays in the plan | T-4d |
| **FFIR** | How many aircrew per airframe, per group, have signed up? | Package size; whether to drop a priority-B target | T-72h |
| **FFIR** | Are both tanker tracks crewed (or AI-flown)? | Range of the strike package | T-72h |

**7.2 Ways to collect in DCS**

- **Tacview review** of the last event. Fastest and cheapest confirmation: were the SAM, the radar, and the bridge actually destroyed?

- **Dedicated recon or armed reconnaissance flights**. A two-ship with a targeting pod, or an AI/remote recon asset if your mission supports one. Give the flight the PIR, not a vague "go look."

- **Designer intel release.** The designer (or the intel lead working from the mission file) releases threat information at a stated **confidence** and **age**: "as of Cycle 6, high confidence." Never release perfect knowledge for free.

- **Pre-strike and post-strike collection** assigned per target on the nomination card, so BDA is planned rather than hoped for.

**DCS NOTE: PIRs and ALR are linked**

**PIRs buy you the right to set a low ALR.** The AFTTP ALR model ties each risk level to confidence in the enemy order of battle: low and moderate ALR assume high confidence in threat locations; significant assumes reasonable confidence; high accepts low confidence.

If ISR could not answer the PIRs, you do not actually have the information to declare a low ALR. Either collect more or set the ALR honestly.

**8. Acceptable Level of Risk (ALR)**

ALR is the CFACC's statement of how much **risk to forces** the mission is worth. It expresses risk to mission and risk to force as an inverse relationship: the more the objective matters, the more force risk the commander may accept, even with no specific threat present (Seymour, drawing on AFTTP 3-3.IPE). It lets tactical leaders make risk decisions without asking for permission each time.

**8.1 Levels (OPAR SPINS 5.2)**

| **ALR** | **Definition** | **Air-to-ground tactics** | **A/A merge ratio** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LOW** | Withdraw to preserve forces. Only favorable engagements. Losses only at training-level attrition. | No single-ship FLOT crossing. Enter SAM/AAA WEZ only with fully effective SEAD. No low-level or reattacks (fixed wing). | 2:1 or better |
| **MEDIUM** | Losses at historical combat rates (about 25%). Accept neutral or disadvantageous engagements. May withdraw to avoid heavy losses. | AAA/MANPADS WEZ as required. SAM envelopes with partially effective SEAD. One reattack authorized. | 1:1 |
| **HIGH** | Accept major losses (about 50%) to achieve the objective. Preserve future capability if able. | Single-ship FLOT crossing authorized. SAM envelopes with marginally effective SEAD. Reattack as required; withdraw if the threat is overwhelming. | 1:2 |
| **EXTREME** | Accept any losses necessary. Recovery is not a factor. | Mission may only be cancelled by higher authority (AWACS/AOC). | Any |

SEAD effectiveness (fully, partially, or marginally effective) is defined in the OPAR SPINS and drives which A/G row applies. The A/A merge ratio uses friendlies to peer adversaries inside Factor Range (25 nm suggested for planning).

**8.2 Choosing an ALR for the cycle**

Ask two questions and write both answers in the AOD:

- **How much do we need this?** Use the priority grade. Higher priority permits higher ALR.

- **How much do we need these aircrew next event?** Sustainment matters. In a group, the scarce resource is player time and morale, not airframes. A wipe on the first push ends someone's night.

| **Priority grade** | **Default ALR** | **Notes** |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | HIGH (EXTREME only for a true must-not-fail effect) | State it explicitly; MCs may accept disadvantageous engagements |
| **B** | MEDIUM to HIGH | HIGH when SEAD enables an A target |
| **C** | MEDIUM | |
| **D** | LOW | Backup targets never justify losses |

*Group adaptation, not doctrine.* Functional defaults in the AFTTP standard are: high-value airborne assets (tankers, AWACS) low, strikers moderate, SEAD and offensive counter-air significant, defensive counter-air moderate (high when defending a designated asset), CSAR moderate. Map "moderate" to MEDIUM and "significant" to MEDIUM-HIGH. Put any per-function deviation in Combined AOD Part 2.

**8.3 How ALR is used**

- **Before execution:** MCs test the mission against the assigned ALR. If it is unlikely to succeed, they tell the CFACC before the event. The CFACC then chooses: **scrub** for a mission that fits, **add assets** or change the plan while staying in ALR, or **raise the ALR** with the same assets and plan (OPAR SPINS 5.1).

- **In flight:** MCs and flight leads use the ALR boundaries to accept or decline a merge, decide on attack or reattack from real-time SEAD effectiveness, and abort when ALR is or will be exceeded.

**BEST PRACTICE: Ceiling, not target**

Even with a HIGH ALR, keep mitigating. The ALR is a ceiling, not a target. Package SEAD, escort, and deception whenever forces allow, and stay well inside the ceiling (Seymour).

**DECISION POINT: Define "loss"**

Decide at the start of the campaign whether aircrew may respawn, and how many times, and what the ALR loss rates mean in that context (percent of aircraft lost, or percent of flights losing a member). A HIGH ALR that assumes 50% losses means nothing if everyone respawns for free.

**8.4 The coalition floor**

The ALR and ROE the CFACC publishes in Part 2 are a floor for the whole coalition. A group may set a tighter (lower-risk) standard for its own aircrew and may always do so without asking. A group may not set a looser one without CFACC approval (Charter Section 10, Part 2). A group's MCs apply whichever is tighter. If a requirement cannot be flown inside the floor at the group's tighter ALR, that is a feasibility call (Section 8.3): the LNO says so before the line is Bought, or the CFACC re-scopes it. It is not a refusal to argue.

**9. Rules of Engagement and the ATO**

**9.1 ROE overlay**

Real ROE come from higher authority and should be published **separately**, not buried in SPINS (AFDP tasking cycle). The Standing Rules of Engagement (CJCSI 3121.01B) are permissive: unless a weapon or tactic is restricted by an approved supplemental measure, commanders may use any lawful means for the mission. Commanders may restrict further, and inherent unit and individual **self-defense** against hostile act or hostile intent is never removed.

For DCS, use the OPAR structure: **WEAPONS FREE**, **TIGHT**, or **HOLD** (formal order required), with defaults by area. The CFACC overrides the default for the ATO day in the AOD. Below that: AWACS, then an MC only in the absence of AWACS, then a flight lead only in the absence of both. State the override in Combined AOD Part 2, and repeat it in the ATO.

Self-defense language should appear verbatim in the ROE: nothing in the ROE negates the right of individual or unit self-defense.

The ROE and ALR are the coalition floor (Section 8.4). The strictness of collateral damage criteria follows the Theater Bible dial sheet (Charter Annex C.4). Record this cycle's setting in Part 2 rather than editing the Bible.

**9.2 Building the ATO**

**DECISION POINT: ATO scope**

The CFACC does not publish a campaign-wide ATO. The tooling creates one draft ATO line per requirement (Tasked Unit blank) so each line can be Bought, and each claiming group's designers build the tasking package for their own mission. The CFACC approves and publishes the final FragOrder, comm plan, and ATO lines. Use the table below as a checklist for what a mission's tasking package should carry. Standing SPINS, the comm matrix, and the ACO are published once per campaign.

The JIPTL becomes packages (the MAAP), and the MAAP becomes the ATO. Doctrine calls for each package to be sequenced and deconflicted in **time, space, and effect**, with support (tankers, EW, ISR) packaged with the strike assets they support.

The USMTF ATO structure is a useful checklist even without the message syntax. It requires effective period, tasked unit, and mission data, plus exactly one of mission location, target location, or reconnaissance data.

| **ATO element** | **What we need per mission** | **USMTF set** |
|---|---|---|
| **Effective period** | Event date and time in Zulu **and** game time (OPAR SPINS 2.1) | PERID |
| **Tasked unit** | Group, flight callsign, aircrew | TASKUNIT |
| **Mission data** | Mission number, package ID, callsign, number and type of aircraft, mission type, alert status, loadout code, IFF/SIF mode and code | MSNDAT |
| **Target / location** | Time on and off target, target ID and type, desired mean point of impact, air support request number | TGTLOC / MSNLOC / RECDATA |
| **Control** | Controlling agency callsign, primary and secondary frequency, report-in point | CONTROL |
| **Air refueling** | Tanker callsign, track, refuel control point and time, altitude, planned offload | REFUEL |
| **Electronic combat** | Callsign, station time, frequencies | ELECMBT |

**Standard conventional loads (SCL)**

The ATO guide uses a five-character code so designers and pilots stop arguing about loadouts. **Air-to-air:** X, then counts of long-, medium-, and short-range missiles, then tanks (X6201 is 6 AIM-120, 2 AIM-7, 0 AIM-9, 1 tank). **Air-to-ground:** count, weapon code, tanks (682H3 is 6 Mk-82 high drag and 3 tanks; 2A652 is 2 AGM-65 and 2 tanks). The recommended loads come from the weapons-system expert; if no code exists for what you want, write it down early so it is in place before planning starts. Have airframe leads maintain a short SCL sheet per airframe.

**BEST PRACTICE: Load follows effect**

Recommended loads should follow the desired effect, weather, and guidance. Tie the load to the effect verb: a "Neutralize during the window" SEAD effect needs different weapons than "Destroy the relay permanently."

**Other planning products (from OPAR SPINS)**

- **Flight plans** filed before every mission: departure, route, arrival, and tasking (OPAR SPINS 3.1.1). They let AWACS and ATC deconflict.

- **Joint Tactical Air Support Request** (JTAS): the standard way to request air support in flight. See the group's own C2 TTP for the format.

- **Killboxes and ROZs** for engagement without terminal attack control (active or closed, owned by a flight). Assign them in the ATO.

- **Time-sensitive targeting matrix** published daily with the prioritized target list (Section 10).

**9.3 Buy/Pass: distributing requirements**

Requirements are not assigned by default. Each approved line posts to the channel of every registered group that has the required airframe, and only those channels. The group's LNO (or a named delegate) selects Buy or Pass. Combat Plans tracks the line through the five statuses below (Charter Section 13.4).

| **Status** | **Meaning** | **Set by** |
|---|---|---|
| **Draft** | Combat Plans is building the comm plan, ACO, or ATO line. Not visible to any group. | Combat Plans |
| **Posted** | Distributed to every registered group for the required airframe. Open for Buy / Pass. | C4/IM tooling |
| **Claimed** | One group has bought it. Withdrawn from other groups' open boards. Inside the 15-minute confirmation window. | First Buy logged |
| **Confirmed** | Window closed. Tasked Unit field set. Locked to the claiming group. | Automatic |
| **Published** | Included in the final mission posting to all participating groups. | CFACC approves |

**Decision rules (Charter Section 13.3)**

- **First Buy wins.** The claim goes to the Buy the tool logs first by server-received timestamp, not by when a member clicked. A dead-even tie is decided by the CFACC on the spot, favoring the group that has filled fewer requirements this cycle.

- **15-minute confirmation window.** The LNO may withdraw a mistaken claim at no cost. After that the line is Confirmed and can be released only through the AOC Board.

- **No one buys.** The line is flagged to the Board and CFACC at T-48h for direct assignment or descoping, which leaves a directly assigned group a full day before T-24h execution planning.

- **Multi-group packages.** Lines needing more than one group post separately under one shared Package ID. Each is claimed independently; Combat Plans groups them in the final ATO and brief.

- **Who may Buy.** Only the group's LNO or a named delegate, on the channel for the required airframe.

**10. Execution, Retasking, and TST**

Execution planning is the last twelve hours before the ATO period in real doctrine; force execution is the period itself. Unit commanders "have the freedom and flexibility to plan missions and delivery tactics as long as they fall within timing requirements, ROE, commander's intent, and create desired effects."

**10.1 What the CFACC does during execution**

- **Watch, do not fly the mission.** Follow the C2 channel or AWACS picture. Answer ALR feasibility and decision-point calls.

- **Retask through AWACS.** In OPAR, AWACS holds retasking authority during execution using an eight-line retasking brief (task, killbox and status, enemy situation, threat, friendlies, SCAR, ordnance restrictions, remarks). At combined scope, a retask that moves a Claimed requirement off its target or away from the claiming group needs that group's LNO concurrence first (Charter Sections 4 and 5.3). Inside pre-briefed decision points, AWACS retasks as before.

- **Act at pre-briefed decision points** (Combined AOD Part 2), not on impulse.

- **Notify Combat Operations.** Any redirection by the MC or AWACS gets reported so the picture and the assessment stay right.

**10.2 Time-sensitive targets**

A TST located in flight takes precedence over other tasking and resources go to neutralizing it as soon as possible. The CFACC publishes the **TST matrix**, with valid TSTs, desired effect, and accepted risk level, alongside the prioritized target list. Keep it short: a mobile SAM, a HVT command vehicle, or a high-payoff logistics node. If everything is a TST, nothing is.

**COMMON ERROR: CFACC in the ear**

The CFACC gets on the radio, bypasses the MC, and rewrites tactics mid-flight. This kills decentralized execution and teaches MCs to stop planning. Set intent and risk in the AOD and let AWACS run the fight.

**11. Assessment: Closing the Loop**

Assessment is "much more than traditional battle damage." Planning for it begins before operations, runs through planning and execution, and each level feeds the one above. It also produces the next cycle's PIRs.

| **Level** | **Question** | **Measure** | **Source** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tactical** | Did the mission execute as tasked? | MOP: weapons on target, TOT met, package intact | MC debrief, AAR, Tacview |
| **Operational** | Did the desired effect occur and persist? | MOE: condition in the objective sentence met | BDA, PIR answers, status board |
| **Campaign** | Are we closer to the End State? Is it time to change air phase? | Air Phase transition criteria in the Intent | CFACC summary, CFC |

**11.1 Reporting**

Use the OPAR structures. **In-flight report:** callsign, mission number, target location, time on target, results (BDA), remarks. **After-action report:** event sequence, lessons learned, and supplements for BDA, intel, and attachments. If ordnance was delivered, BDA is due; if damage is suspected but not confirmed, label it **unconfirmed**. Report enemy maneuver forces at platoon, company, or battalion level rather than single vehicles; single vehicles are appropriate for SAMs, rocket artillery, and special equipment.

Locations must use the SPINS position format so the intel lead can paste them straight into planning tools: N DD MM.MMM E DDD MM.MMM, or MGRS (NNL LL NNNN NNNN). Post BDA and AARs to #combined-aar-bda so ISR collates one combined picture.

**11.2 BDA confidence and re-attack**

| **BDA status** | **Meaning** | **Use** |
|---|---|---|
| **Confirmed** | Sensor, video, or Tacview evidence of the effect | Update status board; close the target |
| **Probable** | Strong indicators, not verified | Assign a PIR to confirm; do not close |
| **Unconfirmed** | Damage suspected, no evidence | Treat as active until collected |

**BEST PRACTICE: Re-attack discipline**

A re-attack is not a reflex. It goes back through the three-question test: is the effect still required, and does it still have to happen this cycle? "It survived" is not a reason by itself.

**COMMON ERROR: Blue fever**

Optimistic BDA. Crews report "target destroyed" from a single pass, the status board goes green, and next event the SAM is still shooting. Require evidence and use the three-status system above.

**12. Making Effects Stick in DCS**

A campaign is a chain of separate DCS missions. Nothing carries over unless someone carries it. This is the biggest practical difference from the doctrine, which assumes the world persists. At combined scope, the Combined Campaign Intent names the persistence method, and the group that hosts the mission file or persistence tool applies the kill list for everyone.

**12.1 The status board**

Keep one **Target System Status Board** (Annex C). It is the campaign's memory and the human-readable ledger the intel lead maintains. Each row shows the target or system, function, status, confidence, when and how it was last confirmed, and whether the change has been applied to the next mission file. At combined scope it is one coalition-wide record, and it is a hand-over item at rotation.

**12.2 Applying the kill list**

- **Manual:** the designer removes confirmed-destroyed units from the next mission file and adjusts degraded ones. Simple, reliable, labor-heavy. The status board is the checklist.

- **Scripted persistence:** if you use a persistence framework or campaign tool, the status board mirrors its state so the CFACC can read it without opening the mission editor.

**DECISION POINT: Persistence method**

Choose your persistence method **before** the campaign starts and write it into the Intent. It decides which verbs are honest: with manual carry-over you can still use Destroy, but a "Degrade" of a complex site is only as real as the designer's time.

**DCS NOTE: Fog and age**

Enemy forces also move and rebuild. Give each intel item an age, and have the designer rebuild or relocate mobile systems (SAM batteries, convoys) between events according to a simple rule (for example, mobile SAMs relocate 5 to 15 nm per cycle). That is what makes PIRs matter.

**13. Common Failures and Time Budget**

| **Failure** | **Symptom** | **Fix** |
|---|---|---|
| **Target tourism** | Targets picked because they are nearby or new | Three-question test on every nomination; keep the "not this cycle" list |
| **AOD written after targets** | AOD exists to justify a chosen list | Combined AOD Part 1 is due before nominations open |
| **PIRs skipped** | Routes and timing picked at random; surprise SAMs | No planning until PIR answers are in or explicitly waived by the CFACC |
| **Undeclared ALR** | Every group or MC assumes its own risk level | ALR floor by function in every Combined AOD; MCs report infeasibility pre-event |
| **Designer-as-CFACC** | Designer picks targets and effects | Separate the hats; designers build only approved targets |
| **Effects vanish** | Destroyed sites are back next mission | Status board plus kill-list step at each cycle |
| **Micromanagement** | AOD dictates tactics; CFACC on the radio | CFACC sets what, why, and risk; MCs own how |
| **Everything is priority A** | No one can tell what matters | Limit A targets per cycle; require a Q3 answer |
| **Tooling accepted unedited** | The Combined JIPTL or ATO goes out exactly as generated, unconnected to this cycle's objective | Every draft is a draft: objective sentence and three-question test before release |
| **CFACC commands a group** | Taskings treated as binding; tactics dictated to a group that Bought a line | Negotiate through the LNO. For a claimed requirement the CFACC's job ends at the Combined AOD |
| **CFC in the weeds** | CFC reviews every cycle's Combined JIPTL | CFC is reactive only (Section 3.3). Set the Intent once |
| **Stale registration or dial sheet** | Lines posted for airframes nobody has; cycles run long | LNOs update the Registration Record; dial changes noted in Part 2 |

**CFACC weekly time budget (a volunteer with a day job)**

| **Task** | **Time** |
|---|---|
| **Read assessment, update "what changed"** | 20 min |
| **Write objective and PIRs (Combined AOD Part 1)** | 10 to 15 min |
| **Review nominations against the three questions** | 20 to 30 min |
| **Write Combined AOD Part 2 (apportionment, ALR, ROE, TST, decision points)** | 15 to 20 min |
| **Monitor Buy/Pass; resolve ties and T-48h unfilled lines** | 10 to 15 min |
| **Approve ATO; handle freeze-line exceptions** | 15 min |
| **Watch execution; make pre-briefed calls** | Event length |

These times are estimates, not measurements. Without tooling they add up to roughly 1.5 to 2 hours before execution. With the tooling drafting the products, aim for under 90 minutes (Charter Annex D.1 expects well under a group-level JFACC's own weekly budget).

**Annex E: Key Tasks, Air Phase Criteria, and Weight of Effort**

*Aligned with the Combined Campaign Intent. The CFC owns the Intent and approves each air phase transition. The CFACC recommends the exit-criteria option and each transition, and adjusts weight of effort within the limits below. These starter values are for the combined campaign; a group's own Intent may use its own.*

**E.1 Design rule**

Key Tasks are **standing and ranked**. An air phase only decides which of Tasks 1 to 4 is the **main effort** this cycle. Task 1 is the main effort in Air Phase 1 and a standing floor afterward, because air superiority is never won permanently. **Task 5 is continuous**: it is never sorted into an air phase or a target tier.

**Air Phases vs. joint phases.** Air Phases 1 to 4 sequence air effort once combat is underway, mostly inside joint Phase III (Dominate), though an opening air-superiority fight can begin in joint Phase II (Seize Initiative). The starting joint phase is chosen in Stage A of the Campaign Cycle document and is a prompt for the opening ALR, not a rule.

**E.2 Key Tasks**

| **#** | **Key Task** | **Target focus** |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Achieve and maintain air superiority** (main effort in Air Phase 1; standing floor after) | Enemy fighter bases, SAM/AAA sites threatening corridors, GCI/EWR nodes |
| **2** | **Interdict lines of communication** | Bridges, rail, key roads, choke points on main supply routes |
| **3** | **Destroy command and control nodes** | HQs, comm relays, radar/C2 facilities not already served under Task 1 |
| **4** | **Attrit rear echelon elements** | Depots, staging areas, maintenance/logistics hubs, reserve formations |
| **5** | **Assist front-line units during maneuvers** (continuous, on call) | Responsive CAS to friendly ground forces in contact or maneuvering |

**DECISION POINT: How Task 5 works**

Task 5 has no target tier. It draws from the CAS share of the weight of effort. **Troops-in-contact CAS is a standing TST, pre-authorized at ALR HIGH,** and interrupts whichever air phase is being worked.

Pre-planned strike and CAS are not committed against an undegraded IADS. Troops-in-contact is the exception because it is a response, not a plan.

**E.3 Constraints (force preservation)**

Force preservation is a **standing constraint**, not a task. It lives in three places in the Intent: the default ALR by air phase, the ALR-by-function matrix (Section 8), and a **loss tripwire** that forces a pause. Define what counts as a "loss" (respawn rules; percent of aircraft or percent of flights) before Cycle 1.

**E.4 Air Phase exit criteria: pick one option**

The CFACC recommends one option for the whole campaign before Cycle 1, and the CFC records it in the Combined Campaign Intent. The CFACC recommends each air phase transition; the CFC approves it. Options 1 and 2 count confirmed BDA only. All numbers and cycle counts are starting values, not doctrine; tune them to your map.

| **Air Phase** | **Option 1: Percent of baseline** | **Option 2: Named effects** | **Option 3: Time-boxed** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Air Superiority (contested)** | Corridor SAMs at or below **20%** of baseline count. EWR coverage of the corridor degraded. No enemy fighter sorties for **2 consecutive events**. | The 2 to 3 SAM batteries the CFACC names are destroyed or neutralized. The strike package flies the corridor with no SAM launch or SAM-caused loss. | **2 cycles.** Exit when the package flew the corridor with losses inside ALR. One extension cycle if not. |
| **2. LOC Interdiction** | At least **75%** of listed choke points confirmed Destroyed or Denied and still down. Convoy traffic below baseline. | The named primary supply route is cut at **2 or more points** and stays unusable for 1 event (confirmed by recon or BDA). | **2 cycles.** Exit when the primary supply route is confirmed cut at 1 or more points. |
| **3. C2 Targeting** | At least **75%** of named nodes and relays Destroyed or Degraded. Enemy reaction measurably slower. | The named HQ and primary relay are destroyed. The campaign designer confirms the enemy reaction rule is degraded (for example, delayed scrambles). | **2 cycles.** Exit when the named HQ is confirmed hit. |
| **4. Rear Echelon Attrition** | At least **80%** of the rear echelon baseline list, or the end-state trigger. | The named fuel and ammo depots are destroyed and the End State conditions are met. | **2 cycles**, then the campaign ends. |

| **Option** | **Best when** | **Cost** |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Percent of baseline** | ISR keeps a clean status board | Most bookkeeping; removes arguments |
| **2. Named effects (recommended for a small staff)** | You want objective exits without percentage tracking | CFACC names the key targets once, up front |
| **3. Time-boxed** | Attendance is unpredictable | An air phase can end with its job unfinished |

**E.5 Standing weight of effort**

Each air phase has a standing split. The CFACC may move any category up to the swing threshold (default 15 points) from the air phase default, with a one-line reason in Combined AOD Part 2. Changes must still total 100%. A shift larger than the threshold needs the CFC. Part 2 then splits the result by group. The percentages apply to the sorties actually signed up across groups, so count sign-ups first.

| **Air Phase** | **Standing weight of effort** | **Default ALR** |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Air Superiority (contested)** | CAP/Sweep 45% • SEAD 30% • Strike (airfields/IADS) 20% • CAS/Other 5% | SEAD HIGH; strikers MEDIUM |
| **2. LOC Interdiction** | Strike (LOCs) 45% • CAP (maintain) 25% • SEAD (residual/reactive) 15% • CAS 15% | MEDIUM |
| **3. C2 Targeting** | Strike (C2) 40% • CAP 20% • SEAD 15% • CAS 25% | MEDIUM |
| **4. Rear Echelon Attrition** | Strike (rear echelon) 40% • CAS 30% • CAP 20% • SEAD 10% | MEDIUM; HIGH for A-grade |

- **CAS is a floor, not a cap.** A troops-in-contact call can pull sorties from another category mid-cycle, even if that pushes CAS above the programmed percentage.

- **Keep a standing CAP/sweep floor** (20 to 25% in Air Phases 2 to 4) so the enemy cannot contest air superiority back while effort shifts.

**E.6 Transition rules**

- **CFACC recommends; CFC approves** every air phase transition. Record it in the AOD. (Charter Section 8 and D.4.)

- **Review limit (Options 1 and 2).** If an air phase is not met in **4 cycles**, the CFACC briefs the CFC and recommends one of three options: raise ALR, add assets, or accept the criteria as met. Option 3 is already time-boxed, with one extension cycle per air phase.

- **Reversion trigger.** The main effort returns to Task 1 automatically if the IADS rebuilds above the Air Phase 1 threshold (Option 1: above 20% of baseline; Option 2: a named battery is back in action; Option 3: a SAM-caused loss in the corridor), or if the loss tripwire is breached.

- **Overlap.** The CFC may approve an air phase started early while Air Phase 1 criteria stay held. Sequential is safer for a small group.

**E.7 Decide before Cycle 1**

- **Exit-criteria option (CFACC recommends, CFC publishes) and the named items if Option 2.**

- **Loss tripwire.** What rate forces a pause? Example: more than 30% of participating aircrew lost in one event, or two consecutive under-attended events. It must match your definition of a "loss."

- **Apportionment swing threshold. Default 15 points; a larger swing goes to the CFC.**

- **Campaign length.** Option 3 gives roughly 8 events. Options 1 and 2 can run 12 to 16. Merge air phases for a shorter campaign.

- **Persistence method and frozen baseline list** (Section 12).
