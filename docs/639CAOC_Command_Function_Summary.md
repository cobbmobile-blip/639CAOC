# 639 CAOC — Command Function Summary

## Why this document exists

The 639 CAOC's Charter (now v0.8, Section 5 and Annex D) describes a command structure sized for a real volunteer staff of 3 people plus the AOC/LNO Board, for part-time DCS players who will not spend heavy time on process. This document is the plain-language companion to that structure and the process it runs.

## The core staff (3 people)

Three seats, not six divisions. A seat is a person, not a department. Those functions still happen; they happen as quick checks these three people make on tool-generated drafts, not as staff work they produce from scratch.

| Seat | Model | What they actually do |
|---|---|---|
| **CFC** | Runs like a small company's CEO | Responsible for the CAOC running, period — not for personally setting campaign intent or grading targets. Makes sure the "machine" (Intent → AOD → Package Plan → ACO/Comm Plan → ATO → mission → BDA) keeps turning. Steps in only when something breaks: a Board deadlock, a new group joining, a major campaign decision. Reactive, not a weekly job. |
| **Chief of Staff** | CFC's second-in-command | Backs up the CFC — covers when the CFC is unavailable, and is the first call if the CFC needs a second set of eyes before a decision. Day to day, shadows what the CFC is responsible for rather than owning a separate lane. |
| **CFACC** | A liaison agent, not a division supervisor | Makes sure the products the tools generate (the AOD, Package Plan, ATO) actually fit what the mission planners need. Chairs the AOC Board. The "quick check that products look correct, and get out to the mission planners" is the CFACC's seat more than anyone's. |

**CFACC's own underlings:** not yet identified. The CFACC may eventually have one or more people helping with that liaison/quick-check function, but who and how many is deliberately left open for now rather than invented here.

## The AOC Board

One LNO per participating group. **The Board exists to make sure each group is getting what it wants out of the process** — a satisfaction check, not a staff-review body.

- Chaired by the CFACC.
- The CFC is the tie-breaker if the Board can't reach consensus.
- No tasking is binding on a group without that group's LNO concurring.

## What's deliberately not here yet

- **No ISR department.** BDA/assessment gets rolled up as a quick check by the CFACC rather than run by a dedicated division. May be added later if the coalition's needs grow into it.
- **CFACC's underlings** — open, not yet identified.
- **Automation.** The intent is to automate as much of the process below as possible via the Intent, AOD, Package Plan, and Comm Grid Builder tools. That automation isn't finished. Until it is, "quick check" sometimes still means more manual review than the long-term design intends.

## The quick-check model

Everyone here is doing a quick check, not deep staff work. The whole point of the tooling is to produce a correct-looking draft so a human only has to glance at it and either approve it or flag what's wrong, then get it out to the mission planners. A "quick check" means: does this product look right, does it serve what the group actually asked for, and is there anything obviously broken before it goes out — not re-deriving the product from scratch or auditing every line against doctrine.

## Who checks what, across the 9-stage process

| Stage | Tool / method | Who quick-checks it | What the check actually is |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Combined Campaign Intent | Intent Builder | CFC (Chief of Staff backs up) | Does the End State and Key Task order still match what the coalition agreed to? Rare. |
| 2. Combined AOD | AOD Builder | CFACC | Does the drafted objective, PIRs, and apportionment reflect this cycle's actual priorities? |
| 3. JIPTL | Embedded in the AOD | CFACC | Quick pass against the three-question test (Key Task, effect, why now). |
| 4. Package Plan (MAAP) | Package Plan Builder | CFACC (or claiming group's LNO) | Does the package match the requirement it's filling? |
| 5. ACO + Theater Comm Plan | Comm Grid Builder / manual DKS overlay | CFACC | Comm plan is standing and rarely revised; the ACO is checked by whoever builds that mission's miz. |
| 6. ATO | Compiled from the above | CFACC | Does the tasking line make sense before it goes out? |
| 7. Mission Planning and Execution | Each group, their own process | Not CAOC staff | Outside this model entirely. |
| 8. BDA / Debrief | Reported by all groups | CFACC rolls it up (no ISR function) | Is the BDA complete enough to feed next cycle's AOD? |

The Board sits outside this table — it's the standing "are we getting what we want" review that runs alongside every cycle.
