ACM Reference ▸
Plain-language definitions for the measure types above, drawn from open-source joint and Army airspace-control doctrine. Not a substitute for the current ACO's own definitions — always use what the ACO itself specifies for a live campaign.
ROZ — Restricted Operating Zone
Airspace of defined dimensions set aside for a specific unit or operation (a tanker track, a SEAD stack, a CAS holding area) so other aircraft stay clear of it without further coordination. Established by the airspace control authority and published in the ACO. JP 3-52, Joint Airspace Control; FM 3-52 App B
Restricted Area
Airspace with specific restrictions on activity for safety, security, or operational reasons. Entry is only as authorized in the ACO — broader and often more persistent than a single-unit ROZ. JP 3-52; AFDP 3-52, Airspace Control
FSCL — Fire Support Coordination Line
A line set by the appropriate land or amphibious force commander. Forward of it, any component may engage surface targets without further coordination with that commander (subject to other active ACMs); short of it, fires must be coordinated with the force controlling that ground. It is a permission boundary, not just a reference line. FM 3-52 App B; FM 90-31 Ch.5
FLOT — Forward Line of Own Troops
A line marking the most forward positions of friendly ground forces at a given time. Used for deconfliction and situational awareness — unlike the FSCL, crossing it carries no automatic coordination requirement by itself. FM 3-52 App B
Arty Firing Lane
A corridor reserved for indirect-fire trajectories (artillery, MLRS/rockets) between a firing position and its target area. Aircraft are routed clear of it, or transit only with coordination, while it is active — conceptually one of the ACO's airspace coordinating measures tied to a surface fire mission. JP 3-52; FM 3-52 App B
ACA — Airspace Coordination Area
A three-dimensional block of airspace, with a floor and ceiling, set aside so friendly aircraft can operate at low level at the same time surface fires are passing through the area — the classic measure for letting CAS and artillery work the same patch of sky safely. JP 3-52; FM 3-52 App B
HIDACZ — High-Density Airspace Control Zone
Airspace with a concentrated volume of traffic or a high density of airspace users, requiring additional coordinating measures beyond the norm — typically drawn around a major air assault, a large CAS stack, or a congested corridor. JP 3-52; FM 3-52 App B
ADIZ — Air Defense Identification Zone
Airspace over land or water in which the ready identification, location, and control of civil and military aircraft is required in the interest of national security — usually large, theater-scale, and drawn once per campaign rather than per mission. JP 3-52
MRR — Minimum Risk Route
A temporary lateral corridor recommended for use by high-speed, fixed-wing aircraft that minimizes their exposure to friendly fires — a flight-planning route, not a fire-support measure. FM 3-52 App B
SAAFR — Standard-Use Army Aircraft Flight Route
A route established below the coordinating altitude for conducting Army aviation traffic, normally without further coordination with the airspace control authority — the rotary-wing counterpart to an MRR. FM 3-52 App B
FFA / NFA / RFA — Fire Area Measures
Three related surface-fire control areas drawn on the same polygon tool here, distinguished by the "Measure type" picker when saving: a Free Fire Area (FFA) is any area into which any weapon system may fire without additional coordination; a No Fire Area (NFA) is one into which no fires or their effects are allowed, with narrow exceptions; a Restricted Fire Area (RFA) has specific restrictions beyond the norm and requires coordination before fires that do not meet those restrictions. FM 3-52 App B; FM 90-31 Ch.5
Coordinating altitude / coordination level — not a drawn shape, but the backbone of the ACO: the altitude (or flight level) at or below which aircraft normally operate under positive or procedural control measures like these, and above which a different control scheme (usually positive, radar-based) applies. Set it once per campaign under Theater Parameters above.
Tactical control measures below (not formal JP 3-52 ACM categories, but standard working-ACO/overlay fixtures):
Bullseye
A fixed geographic reference point used for brevity — bearing/range calls, target handoffs, threat calls — instead of lat/lon. Universal in modern air-to-air and strike brevity, but not itself an ACM in the joint airspace-control sense. common tactical usage
CAP / AWACS Station
A working holding area for a combat air patrol or an AWACS/E-3 orbit. Doctrinally this is usually just a ROZ assigned to that mission, but it is broken out here as its own type so fighter and C2 stations are visually distinct from general-purpose ROZs on a busy board. derived from ROZ, JP 3-52
